Executive summary

Voice UX Lab is an eight-week study of voice-first AI interaction and collaborative development. The team built Ten Minutes, a mobile voice tarot prototype with interactive card selection and personal reports.

As of November 27, basic login and consultation were reported to work in device tests. The team was preparing the submission build; full acceptance, measured voice quality and presentation outcomes are not yet verified.

02 / draft

Problem & research questions

Voice offers a natural medium for a personal conversation, but precise selections and persistent results benefit from a screen. Ten Minutes explores the balance between those two interaction modes.

The team investigated Korean speech naturalness, character consistency, turn-taking, session pacing and the alignment of conversation with application state.

04 / draft

Design & architecture

The application uses a React and TypeScript mobile web frontend, a FastAPI backend, PostgreSQL and Vercel. The browser connects to OpenAI Realtime over WebRTC; model response text is synthesized into speech through Typecast.

Application-controlled actions coordinate card selection and reveal with the conversational agent. The backend manages consultation state, card data and report persistence.

The hybrid approach supports voice selection but requires explicit handling of interruption, playback timing, and reconnection.

05 / draft

Development & technical insights

The team moved from scripted proofs of concept to a structured React and TypeScript application, then integrated voice, card selection and report handling. The design evolved through visual concept review and a complete deck of 78 card illustrations.

Codex supported software development. The team established feature specifications, a monorepo and a Git Flow with feature branches, pull requests, development integration and release checks. Human review was important in refining the voice persona and dialogue.

Evaluation & improvement

Members conducted exploratory service research and prototype tests. Qualitative observations concerned ease of entry, participation in card selection, response pacing, naturalness and closing.

Basic device checks were reported by November 27. These observations do not establish user satisfaction, reliability, or quantitative latency. Further controlled tests are required.

09 / draft

Limitations & next steps

The team has not completed formal evaluation of voice naturalness, latency, interruption handling, full device compatibility or end-to-end reliability. Exploratory research cannot be generalized to the broader market.

Payments, repeated card draws, expanded reader options and broader research remain follow-up work. The November 27 deadline is for submission, not proof of a finished release. The planned presentation is December 16.
