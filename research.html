Study findings

Voice UX research

What we learned while designing a voice-first experience: when to speak, when to listen, and when a screen is better.

Research questions

  • How can a voice agent respond naturally in Korean while maintaining a consistent character?
  • Which interactions are better performed by voice, and which need a visual interface?
  • How can the application keep model responses, card reveal and spoken playback aligned?

Voice architecture trade-offs

The team investigated OpenAI, ElevenLabs and Typecast. The current design combines Realtime speech understanding and conversational reasoning with Typecast speech synthesis.

Direct model audio is a simpler route. A separate synthesis provider gives more control over the voice, but adds latency, cancellation and synchronization work.

ObservationDesign response
Spoken card numbers were difficult to recognize reliably.Use direct touch for precise card selection.
Long explanations felt less conversational.Prefer short utterances and wait for the user’s turn.
Card revelation and speech could fall out of sync.Coordinate reading with application state and reveal events.
A hybrid voice pipeline can add response delay.Evaluate timing, naturalness and interruption behavior together.

Persona and prompt engineering

  • Separate the persona’s identity and values from operational instructions and reference knowledge.
  • Write example utterances for each consultation stage and spread.
  • Use manual editing and review to reduce repetitive, formal or overly elaborate AI phrasing.
  • Use versioned prompts and application-controlled tools to make changes reviewable.
  • Use a human-reviewed reference of 78 cards and connect the revealed card, question and position.

Exploratory service research

Members experienced telephone, in-person and digital tarot services in November. We report only aggregate interaction observations, not personal concerns or individual provider assessments.

ThemeImplication
Availability and entryReduce the friction of arranging a consultation.
Onboarding and contextAsk for the relevant context, not unnecessary personal information.
Card participationLet users take part in the card selection and see the result.
Time constraintsGive an intentional closing rather than an unexpected end.
Voice comfortOffer a low-pressure conversational opening and clear controls.
Persistent resultsGive users an optional report to revisit the experience.

Evaluation plan

The team observed qualitative behavior in exploratory tests. The following measures are proposed for future controlled evaluation; no quantitative benchmark or representative market result is claimed.

MeasureWhat to check
Turn latencyTime from the end of user speech to the start of audible response.
Interruption successWhether the old response stops and the new user turn is handled.
Task completionWhether users complete question clarification, card selection, reading and report.
Persona consistencyWhether speech retains Lena’s character across scenarios.
NaturalnessQualitative rating of pacing, wording and conversational turn-taking.
