    Project selection

    The team reviewed 14 project proposals and selected MIND DETOXING through a vote.

    The closing announcement recorded 5 votes for MIND DETOXING, 2 for the conversational wake-up app, 1 for the running coach, and 1 for Docent. These are the recorded results, not current reaction counts.

    The original concept combines voice conversations at night with reflection reports during the day. It proposes empathetic listening, CBT-informed questions, conversation memory, and daily or weekly summaries.

    Other project ideas

    The following pros and cons are retrospective assessments added to this report. They are not confirmed statements from the meeting or reasons for individual votes.

    Docent: multilingual exhibition guide

    • Pro 1: A clear setting for follow-up questions and multilingual conversation.
    • Pro 2: Official exhibition materials can ground answers.
    • Pro 3: A pilot can start with one exhibit and one tablet.
    • Con 1: Realistic testing needs an exhibition partner.
    • Con 2: Noise and reluctance to speak in public may limit use.
    • Con 3: Content updates and answer checks require ongoing work.

    VoiceQuest: multiplayer voice mystery game

    • Pro 1: Team members can test the game together.
    • Pro 2: The game tests turn-taking, interruptions, and shared context.
    • Pro 3: One short mystery gives the prototype a clear scope.
    • Con 1: Enjoyment depends heavily on story quality.
    • Con 2: Simultaneous speakers make interaction difficult.
    • Con 3: Spoilers or inconsistent clues can break the game.

    VoiceFit: voice-controlled workout assistant

    • Pro 1: Hands-free control addresses a concrete inconvenience.
    • Pro 2: Timers, pauses, and corrections give measurable tasks.
    • Pro 3: User-defined routines reduce the need for specialist content.
    • Con 1: Existing timers may already meet user needs.
    • Con 2: Breathing and background noise can reduce recognition quality.
    • Con 3: Incorrect timing or commands can disrupt exercise.

    Reservation assistant: AI phone bookings

    • Pro 1: The service completes a concrete task.
    • Pro 2: It can reach businesses without online booking.
    • Pro 3: It tests conversation, negotiation, and delegated actions.
    • Con 1: Phone integration adds cost and complexity.
    • Con 2: Staff may decline or struggle with AI calls.
    • Con 3: Ambiguous answers can cause incorrect booking confirmations.

    Voice body double: help with task completion

    • Pro 1: The service addresses difficulty starting and finishing tasks.
    • Pro 2: It tests proactive check-ins and persistent task state.
    • Pro 3: Completion and repeat use give concrete evaluation measures.
    • Con 1: Check-ins may feel intrusive or like nagging.
    • Con 2: Scheduling and reconnection add complexity.
    • Con 3: Use may decline after the initial novelty fades.

    FamilySync: shared family scheduling

    • Pro 1: The service addresses recurring coordination needs.
    • Pro 2: It tests multi-user context and consent.
    • Pro 3: A three-person scenario gives a focused prototype.
    • Con 1: Adoption needs participation from several family members.
    • Con 2: Speaker identification and calendar access add complexity.
    • Con 3: Permission or schedule errors can undermine trust.

    Overtime discussion: everyday reflection

    After the meetup, a few members explored a lighter role than specialized mental healthcare. These ideas did not finalize the product scope.

    A hands-free brain dump could let users speak freely instead of typing, then review an organized account of their day.

    Distance from their thoughts may help users view situations more objectively and notice recurring triggers or patterns. A personal checklist was suggested.

    Users need private time and a space where they can speak comfortably. Some users value writing by hand, so the team must examine when speech is useful.

    People may share worries with a trusted listener who helps them notice assumptions, biases, mistaken judgments, or leaps in reasoning. A diary or spoken account can still be selective or incomplete.

    Open questions and follow-up

    • Will use occur mainly during difficult moments, or become a daily habit?
    • Which output helps reflection: a summary, questions, a pattern checklist, or something else?
    • How should the service balance listening, questions, and alternative interpretations?
    • A simple shareable output was mentioned, but its form remains undefined.
    • Define the initial users, minimum complete experience, service boundaries, roles, and milestones.
